# SYSTEM OVERVIEW
**Purpose:** A systematic thinking framework for analyzing ANY customer-related question,
decision, or strategy from the customer's perspective—from pre-product ideas to scale-stage
challenges.
**Core Principle:** Scenarios are infinite. The thinking process is finite and repeatable.
**What Makes This Different:** This system doesn't just tell you to "think like a customer"—it
provides the exact mental machinery to do so, regardless of your company stage or evidence
availability.
---
# GLOBAL GUARDRAILS
## What This System DOES
- Systematically shifts perspective from founder-view to customer-view
- Calibrates analysis depth to company lifecycle stage
- Diagnoses what type of customer question is being asked
- Handles both evidence-rich and evidence-poor scenarios
- Applies appropriate specialized frameworks based on question type
- Surfaces evidence gaps and assumption risks
- Produces customer-grounded recommendations with calibrated confidence
## What This System DOES NOT DO
- Validate founder assumptions without scrutiny
- Treat all customers as monolithic
- Accept weak evidence as strong signal
- Provide false confidence when evidence is lacking
- Skip the "would customers actually want this?" question
- Give the same analysis regardless of company stage
## Failure Protocol

Page 2 StartupGTM.pro

When customer perspective cannot be reliably determined:
1. Identify what's missing (segment clarity, evidence, context)
2. Apply Cold Start Protocol if evidence is near-zero
3. Formulate specific research questions using Research Methodology
4. Flag confidence level explicitly on any provisional answer
5. Provide validation path before high-stakes decisions
## Devil's Advocate Protocol (DAP)
Apply after EVERY step output:
```
DAP CHECKLIST:
□ Am I actually thinking like the customer, or projecting what I want them to think?
□ Did I identify the RIGHT customers for this question, or the convenient ones?
□ Am I being honest about evidence quality, or rationalizing weak signals?
□ What would a skeptic challenge about this analysis?
□ Would a real customer recognize this as their perspective?
EVALUATION:
- 4-5 boxes checked → PROCEED
- 2-3 boxes checked → REVISE (note what's weak)
- 0-1 boxes checked → MAJOR REVISION (return to earlier step)
```

# COLD START PROTOCOL
## Trigger
Use when:
- Lifecycle Stage = Pre-Product
- Evidence Assessment returns "None" or "Assumption-based only"
- Question is about validating an idea before building
- No customers exist yet to analyze
## Purpose
Provide structured thinking for customer questions when you have no customers or minimal
evidence. Prevents false confidence and guides toward validation.
## Instructions
### CS.1 Hypothetical Customer Construction
Since real customers don't exist or evidence is minimal, construct plausible customer
archetypes:
```
HYPOTHETICAL CUSTOMER ARCHETYPE:
WHO (Best Guess):
- Most likely role/title: [Educated guess based on problem space]
- Most likely context: [Situation they'd be in when experiencing problem]
- Company type/size: [If B2B, what kind of company]
- Source of this guess: [Where did this assumption come from? Personal experience? Industry
knowledge? Analogy to other markets?]
PROBLEM (Hypothesis):
- Problem they likely face: [Specific description]
- Frequency/severity: [How often? How painful?]

Page 6 StartupGTM.pro

- How they likely solve it today: [Current alternative—could be manual process, competitor, or
nothing]
- Why current solution is inadequate: [The gap your solution would fill]
- What triggers them to seek a solution: [The moment they'd look for something new]
MOTIVATION (Hypothesis):
- What would success look like for them: [Their desired outcome]
- What they'd be willing to pay/do: [Investment they'd make]
- What would make them NOT buy/use: [Blockers and objections]
VALIDATION STATUS: ⚠️ UNVALIDATED HYPOTHESIS
- This is an educated guess, not customer knowledge
- Every element above needs validation before confidence
```

### CS.2 Assumption Stacking
List EVERY assumption embedded in this hypothesis. Be ruthlessly honest:
| # | Assumption | Confidence | If Wrong, Impact | How to Test |
|---|------------|------------|------------------|-------------|
| 1 | [The problem exists] | [H/M/L] | [What breaks] | [Quick test] |
| 2 | [Target persona experiences it] | [H/M/L] | [What breaks] | [Quick test] |
| 3 | [Current solutions are inadequate] | [H/M/L] | [What breaks] | [Quick test] |
| 4 | [They'd pay to solve it] | [H/M/L] | [What breaks] | [Quick test] |
| 5 | [They'd pay YOUR price] | [H/M/L] | [What breaks] | [Quick test] |
| 6 | [They'd buy from YOU] | [H/M/L] | [What breaks] | [Quick test] |
| 7 | [Add more as relevant] | [H/M/L] | [What breaks] | [Quick test] |
**Flag Critical Assumptions:** Which assumptions, if wrong, would completely invalidate the
idea?
### CS.3 Minimum Viable Validation Threshold
Before ANY recommendation to proceed, require evidence threshold:
```
VALIDATION THRESHOLD:
LEVEL 1 - Problem Validation (Must have before building):
□ 10+ conversations with target persona
□ 5+ people who describe the problem unprompted (not leading questions)

□ Evidence of current spending (time OR money) on alternatives
□ Problem articulated in THEIR words, not yours
LEVEL 2 - Solution Validation (Must have before scaling):
□ 5+ people who've seen solution and want access
□ 3+ people willing to pay or pre-order
□ At least 1 person who would be "very disappointed" if solution went away
LEVEL 3 - Business Validation (Must have before major investment):
□ Paying customers exist
□ Repeatable acquisition channel identified
□ Unit economics show path to sustainability
CURRENT VALIDATION LEVEL: [0 / 1 / 2 / 3]
THRESHOLD FOR THIS DECISION: [Which level is required]
```

# STEP 1: CUSTOMER MAPPING
## Purpose
Identify WHICH customers are relevant to this question. Never analyze "customers" as monolith.
## Instructions
### 1.1 Segment Identification
For this specific question, identify relevant customer segments:
**PRIMARY SEGMENT:**
- Who is MOST affected by this decision?
- Who will this impact first/most?
- Whose opinion should weight heaviest for THIS question?
**SECONDARY SEGMENT(S):**
- Who else is affected?
- Whose needs might conflict with primary?
- Who might we be ignoring that matters?
**ANTI-SEGMENT (Explicitly NOT for):**
- Who should we explicitly NOT optimize for here?
- Whose feedback would be misleading for this question?
- Who would we be okay disappointing?
### 1.2 Segment Characterization
For each relevant segment, establish:
```
SEGMENT: [Name]
WHO THEY ARE:
- Role/Title: [What they do]
- Context: [Their situation, pressures, constraints, daily reality]
- Sophistication: [Novice / Intermediate / Expert in this domain]
- Decision authority: [Can they buy alone? Need approval? Part of committee?]

Page 10 StartupGTM.pro

WHAT THEY CARE ABOUT:
- Primary motivation: [What drives their decisions in this area]
- Success metric: [How they measure winning—what makes them look good]
- Fear/Risk: [What they're trying to avoid—what makes them look bad]
- Time horizon: [Thinking about today? This quarter? This year?]
HOW THEY BEHAVE:
- Decision process: [Solo / Committee / Influenced by whom]
- Information sources: [Where they learn, who they trust, what they read]
- Current solution: [What they do today without you—the REAL competition]
- Switching triggers: [What would make them change from current solution]
```

### 1.3 Buyer Psychology by Segment

# STEP 2: PERSPECTIVE SHIFT
## Purpose
Actually inhabit the customer's mind. Think FROM their position, not ABOUT them.
## Instructions
### 2.1 Context Immersion
Before analyzing, ground yourself in customer reality. This is not about your product—it's about
their life:
**THE CUSTOMER'S DAY:**
- What did they do in the hour before encountering this question/product/decision?
- What will they do in the hour after?
- What else is competing for their attention RIGHT NOW?
- What pressures are they under that have nothing to do with you?
- What did their boss just ask them about?
- What's keeping them up at night (that isn't your product category)?
**THE CUSTOMER'S KNOWLEDGE:**
- What do they know about this problem/category that you know?

Page 13 StartupGTM.pro

- What do they NOT know that you know? (Your curse of knowledge)
- What do they believe that might be wrong?
- What language do they use vs. what language do you use?
- How sophisticated is their understanding of solutions in this space?
**THE CUSTOMER'S OPTIONS:**
- What else could they do instead of engaging with you?
- What's the "do nothing" option look like? (Often the real competition)
- Who else is trying to get their attention/budget/time?
- What would they have to STOP doing to START doing this?
### 2.2 First-Person Articulation
Speak AS the customer. Write their internal monologue:
```
CUSTOMER VOICE:
"When I think about [topic of the question], I feel [emotion] because [reason].
My day-to-day reality is [description]. The thing I'm actually trying to accomplish is [real goal],
not [what vendors think my goal is].
What I actually need is [real need], but what I usually get from vendors is [current reality /
frustration].
The thing that would make me [take action / buy / switch / engage] is [specific trigger]. I'd need
to see/feel/know [evidence required].
The thing that would make me [hesitate / leave / ignore / reject] is [specific blocker]. My biggest
fear is [fear].
What I would never tell a salesperson but actually think is [hidden truth].
If I'm being honest, the reason I haven't solved this already is [real reason—inertia, priority,
budget, belief it's unsolvable, etc.]."
```
