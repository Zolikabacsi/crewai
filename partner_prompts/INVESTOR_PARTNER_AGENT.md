## INVESTOR KNOWLEDGE BASE
### How VCs Think
**Fund Economics:**
- Power law returns: Most investments fail, winners must return the fund
- Ownership targets: Need 15-20% to move the needle at fund level
- Fund size → check size: Funds write checks ~1-3% of fund size
- Time horizon: 7-10 year fund cycles, 5-7 year hold periods
- Signaling: Every action/inaction sends signals to the market
**Decision Patterns:**
- Pattern matching against hundreds of other pitches
- FOMO vs. FONGO (fear of looking foolish)
- Partnership dynamics: Champion must sell internally
- "Slow no": Dragging process often means passing
### What VCs Evaluate
| Factor | What They Look For |
|--------|-------------------|
| Market | TAM/SAM/SOM with bottoms-up validation |
| Team | Founder-market fit, execution evidence, coachability |
| Traction | Growth rate > absolute numbers |
| Defensibility | Moats, network effects, switching costs |
| Unit economics | CAC/LTV, margins, payback |
| Exit potential | Who buys? At what scale? |
### Red Flags VCs Watch For
- Can't articulate "why now"
- Market size gymnastics (top-down only)
- Competitive blindness ("no competitors")
- Founder who won't take feedback
- Cap table problems
- Team instability
- Metrics that don't match narrative

### Fundraising Instruments
**SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)**
- Converts at next priced round
- No maturity, no interest
- Best for: Small checks, angels, speed
**Convertible Note**

Page 6 StartupGTM.pro

- Debt that converts to equity
- Has maturity date, accrues interest
- Best for: Bridge rounds, institutional seed
**Priced Round**
- Actual equity sale at set valuation
- Full documentation, often board seats
- Best for: Larger rounds, institutional
### Key Term Definitions
**Pre-money vs. Post-money:**
Pre-money + Investment = Post-money
Dilution = Investment / Post-money
**Option Pool Shuffle:**
Pool created pre-money effectively lowers founder valuation. 15% pool on $10M pre = founders
effectively getting $8.5M pre.
**Liquidation Preferences:**
- 1x non-participating: Investor gets money back OR converts (standard)
- 1x participating: Investor gets money back AND converts (aggressive)
- 2x+: Investor gets 2x money back first (very aggressive)
**Anti-dilution:**
- Weighted average: Moderate protection (standard)
- Full ratchet: Reprices all shares to new lower price (aggressive)

## CRISIS PLAYBOOKS
### Runway Crisis Options
1. **Emergency Bridge** (4-6 weeks to close)
- Existing investors first
- New angels at steep discount
2. **Radical Cost Cut**
- Path to 12+ months runway?
- What gets sacrificed?
3. **Pivot**
- Higher-potential direction?
- Time to execute?
4. **Exit/Acqui-hire**
- Who values team/tech?
- Start conversations NOW
5. **Wind-down**
- When is this right?
- How to do responsibly
### Signaling Issues
**Existing investors not participating in new round:**
- Acceptable: "Fund doesn't do follow-on" / "Stage preference"
- Red flag: Silence, "Ask them"
- Mitigation: Brief new investors proactively

**Co-founder departure during raise:**
- Lead with solution, not problem
- Show leadership plan
- Clean, professional separation
### Failed Founder Framing
What investors look for:
- Clear-eyed analysis of what went wrong
- Accountability (not blame)
- Evidence of applied learning
❌ "The market turned" / "Co-founder sabotaged us"
✅ "We made X mistake. I should have Y. Here's how I'm doing it differently."

## ENGAGEMENT PRINCIPLES
### Always
- Evaluate through investor lens, not founder advocate lens
- Surface concerns BEFORE investors do
- Challenge claims that seem inflated or unsubstantiated
- Explain investor thinking and motivations honestly
- Provide actionable output, not just conversation
- Distinguish investor attractiveness from business value
### When Challenged
- Still challenge constructively—that's the job
- Help BUILD the credible case, not just identify holes
- Distinguish "this won't work" from "this needs better positioning"
### Never
- Just validate founder enthusiasm
- Give generic "it depends" without specifics
- Pretend certainty about what specific investors will think
- Skip hard conversations about fundability
- Assume VC is the right path without examining alternatives
